# Syndra: The Living Workshop

Creative treatment and interaction storyboard | 11 October 2026

Implementation follow-up: [The Living Workshop](../../docs/product/living-workshop.md)
documents the production journey and its offline verification. The proposal
and research provenance below are retained as the original design record.

## The Brief

The current Studio is a stronger working interface, but its signature scene is
still an object inside a conventional application. The next direction should
make the opening, middle, transition into work, and execution surfaces feel
like parts of one authored experience.

This is not a request for more animations on the existing layout. It is a
request for composition, narrative, interaction and material craft to carry
through the entire product.

**Creative premise: an intent becomes a visible, inspectable piece of work.**

The signature is an original orange ribbon with angular, Syndra-specific
folds. It begins as a single suspended form, unfolds into a network of work,
meets an explicit scope boundary, and becomes a precise selection rail in the
Studio. Its geometry should borrow from our own monogram, not from another
company's phoenix, astronaut, glass object or coding-agent identity.

This document specifies a direction. It does not claim that the new journey
has been implemented, that public demos represent real runs, or that all
reference-site gestures have been personally tested.

## Research And Evidence

The supplied [Awwwards interactive collection](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/web-interactive/)
is the discovery reference. Its direct web fetch continues to time out. No
browser surface was exposed by the connected browser inventory on this date.
The preceding Windows inspection also stopped because it could not establish

This treatment therefore uses available primary pages, creator explanations
and the previously recorded 8 October public-site captures in
`.tmp/kinetic-research/`. Static captures establish composition, not gesture
behavior. Creator accounts establish intended interaction, not independently
measured performance. Historical award iterations are not assumed identical
to the current live sites.

### Reference Lessons

- [Lusion's account of its third site](https://www.commarts.com/project/36283/lusion)
  describes a protagonist moving through successive environments, with
  reversible scroll and a slowing interaction. The lesson is narrative
  continuity: a visitor can influence one journey instead of encountering
  disconnected reveal effects. This is the creator's historical account.
- [Lusion's Devin case study](https://lusion.co/projects/devin_ai/)
  connects interactive storytelling to making a complex software product
  understandable. Its [archived project](https://archive-devin-ai.lusion.co/)
  progresses from a task example to workspace tools and collaborative uses.
  The lesson is to demonstrate the product's behavior in the middle of the
  experience, not replace explanation with abstract spectacle.
- [Noomo's 2026 creator breakdown](https://noomoagency.com/insights/the-power-of-digital-storytelling-website)
  explains a recurring character, chapter-scale camera changes, a quieter
  content-focused portion and separately authored mobile motion. The lesson
  is to carry one visual idea across changing attention needs. Its account
  also distinguishes deliberate pacing from adding more effects.
- [Unseen's current entry](https://unseen.co/) offers exploration and a
  non-audio entry. The lesson is an invitation to participate, with a
  conventional way to enter, rather than a compulsory intro. Its gesture
  prompts were read; live gestures have not been re-tested here.
- [Clou](https://www.clou.ch/) links its verbal identity, project presentation
  and agency content. The lesson is that typography, illustration and
  editorial arrangement must carry identity alongside any 3D work.

The Syndra treatment below is our inference and original design proposal,
not a reproduction of those sites or their assets.

## One Journey, Three Acts

### Act I: Arrival

**Feeling:** anticipation, physicality, confidence.

Opening composition:

- The headline is `Your intent. Made real.` It sits on an asymmetric, generous
  field with a large ribbon form, not inside a bordered hero card.
- Close material detail is important: believable folded edges, contact
  shadows and controlled lighting. The form should look authored even in a
  still image. The current five-slab stack is not the new hero centerpiece.
- A short arrival reveals the mark and the form together. Content and the
  direct Studio entry are available before the scene has finished loading.
- `Explore how it works` enters the story. `Open Studio` remains a direct
  alternative. There is no mandatory sound, drag, loader percentage or
  waiting ritual.

Interaction:

- Pointer movement makes a small, bounded change to perspective, not to the
  entire page or the user's cursor.
- A native button unfolds the ribbon. Its response changes the composition,
  not just the color of an icon. Keyboard and touch receive the same outcome.
- Scrolling advances the story naturally. Scrolling back restores earlier
  visual states without resetting the page or changing any actual work.

The first transition keeps the same form visible. The camera pulls back, the
fold becomes a path, and the opening type gives way to a concrete task example.
This continuity is the transition, not a fade to an unrelated feature grid.

### Act II: The System Unfolds

**Feeling:** curiosity becomes understanding.

The middle has distinct scenes, not three copies of a split text/image panel.

#### Intent Becomes Structure

An explicitly labelled example reads:

`Fix checkout retries without changing billing.`

The ribbon opens into a small work landscape. A context fragment, a bounded
plan and its expected artifacts occupy different depths. The key visual fact
is the boundary between checkout and billing, not a vague swarm of AI nodes.

Selecting an example such as repair or refactor updates the entire explanatory
scene: task, scope, structure and artifact. The selection is local and cannot
start a provider call. Selection is represented in native HTML as well as the
scene. Examples never pretend to be running jobs or measured product results.

#### Collaboration Becomes Visible

The plan opens into connected pieces of work. A selected piece comes forward;
related context and artifacts become legible while unrelated paths recede.
The same selection remains recognizable through the camera change.

This is not an assertion that every run has the same roles or number of
stages. The explanatory example may show a small graph; production uses the
actual persisted graph, including parallel tasks, waits and retries.

#### Power Meets A Boundary

At the most consequential part of the explanation, the scene stops moving.
The ribbon reaches a clear scope boundary. An opaque, readable operation
surface takes priority: what is requested, where it acts and why it needs a
decision. This is a visual expression of control, not a decorative lock icon.

The public example can demonstrate inspecting a request. It cannot authorize
a real request. Production permission actions remain exact-revision native
controls; swiping, dragging, scrolling and holding a 3D object never approve.

#### Work Leaves Evidence

The abstract paths settle into tangible artifacts: a source change, a check
result and a separately identified review. Each can be inspected rather than
being reduced to a green completion badge.

Composition changes here: less cinematic depth, more precise type and useful
detail. The visual idea remains, but it is no longer competing with reading.
Use a quieter material field within the user's chosen appearance; do not
silently switch their Studio theme for narrative effect.

### Act III: Enter The Workshop

**Feeling:** the promise becomes a usable tool.

The explanatory work surface resolves into the same visual grammar as the
Studio: a work rail, broad artifact plane and clearly separate decision area.
The final website section should already feel related to the real product.
No unrelated login dashboard appears after a cinematic landing page.

The public example is not copied into authenticated state. A visual handoff
does not imply that demonstration files, history or approval records exist in
the user's workspace. Authentication and workspace validation remain real.

Returning sessions and direct run links bypass the story and restore work.
There is no intro replay on resume, reconnect, refresh or approval.

## The Execution Experience

Execution must be equally authored, not the portion where creativity ends.
Its interaction is more precise because people are now making decisions.

| Surface | Creative treatment | Functional contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | A generous intent surface, with scope arranged as a visible frame around it. | Preserve the draft; separate configured providers from verified readiness. |
| Overview | An explorable work landscape, with selection linked to a native task register. | Use persisted tasks and outcomes; missing data remains unavailable. |
| Task to artifact | The selected work plane expands into the readable file surface, preserving its identity. | Immediate response, correct path, stable reading position and deep link. |
| Diff | A broad editorial code surface with precise changed-line emphasis. | Selectable text, virtualized rows, keyboard scrolling and diff signs. |
| Decision | A calm, high-contrast operation plane interrupts the visual flow deliberately. | Exact request/revision; stale conflict cannot resume execution. |
| Verification and review | Two distinguishable evidence surfaces with their own textual outcomes. | Passing tests never imply reviewer acceptance. |
| Replay | A time rail links recorded decisions to their artifacts; selection reveals the record in place. | Historical inspection only; scrubbing cannot rerun tools or mutate the workspace. |
| Failure | Decorative motion settles, the affected path becomes explicit, preserved artifacts remain reachable. | Do not hide side effects, invent rollback or rewrite completed task history. |
| History | A designed archive with strong typographic hierarchy, not a wall of identical cards. | Server paging, honest scope, retained filters and selection. |

A optional spatial view is not the only way to operate. Native list and
artifact views are complete. Opening source or an approval does not retain
an expensive scene behind the text. Users can disable expressive rendering
without losing any action or meaning.

## The Detail Language

- Shape: the ribbon fold, selection rail and edge of an active work plane
  share one angular motif. Do not turn every container into a monogram.
- Type: expressive display typography for arrival; functional typography for
  files, decisions and evidence. Large headlines are not a substitute for
  editorial hierarchy in the middle.
- Material: warm porcelain, graphite, restrained metal and orange identity.
  Reserve contrast and depth for the active subject, not every panel.
- Motion: a shared direction of travel and spring character connects the
  chapters. Larger movement marks a narrative change; ordinary controls
  respond locally. New content must not wait for a transition to finish.
- Feedback: selection, hover, pending server confirmation and terminal
  success are different states. An animated path never fabricates progress.
- Finish: typography, focus, long filenames, error copy, disabled actions,
  loading geometry, menus and empty states are part of the composition.
  Screenshots of a happy hero alone cannot pass design review.
- Audio: not needed for the product. Do not add autoplay sound or make a
  sound cue the only confirmation of an action.

## Mobile Is A Separate Composition

The mobile scene uses fewer visible elements, a different camera arrangement
and shorter transitions. It is not a scaled-down desktop sculpture with
unreadable labels. The ribbon can become an edge-aligned sequence with native
tap controls instead of a wide spatial landscape.

At 320px, the operation, file name and next action remain legible. Touch does
not depend on hover or device-motion permissions. Reduced motion presents the
same chapters with static authored compositions and direct navigation.

## Proof Before Production

1. Build a separate three-act interaction study with clearly labelled sample
   data and no API client. Judge the intro, the middle, the handoff and an
   actual artifact/decision composition together, not just the opening.
2. Validate continuity by moving forward and backward through the sequence.
   Check direct entry, keyboard, touch, reduced motion, renderer failure and
   interrupted animation. Mobile gets its own composed version.
3. Refine the scene's geometry, materials and transitions in the browser.
   Choose rendering tools after establishing the required effect; adding a
   library is not proof of a better experience.
4. Migrate approved compositions to React without importing the sample store.
   Keep typed APIs, event scoping, durable identity, launch authentication,
   exact approvals, bounded lists and navigation memory authoritative.
5. Verify representative real success, rejection, failure, stale approval,
   unavailable data and reconnect, not simulated completion alone. Existing
   offline installed-product acceptance remains mandatory.

Quality and performance targets are acceptance work, not current measurements:
functional entry never waits for the scene; optional rendering is lazy and
offscreen/hidden work stops; dynamic quality and static fallback protect
interaction latency. Measure device behavior before promising frame rates.

## Definition Of A Better Result

The opening should be recognizable in a still frame. The middle should invite
participation and explain something specific. Entering Studio should preserve
the visual idea. Working should remain clear after the novelty wears off.

That is the next bar: **one crafted experience from curiosity to execution,
not an award-style intro attached to a generic application.**
